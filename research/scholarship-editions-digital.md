# Foundational Scholarship, Historical Editions, and Digital Humanities

---

## 1. Foundational Joyce Scholarship (Chronological)

The critical history of *Finnegans Wake* is vast, but several foundational texts laid the groundwork for how the novel is read and understood today.

### Samuel Beckett et al. — *Our Exagmination* (1929)
- **Full title:** *Our Exagmination Round His Factification for Incaminasion of Work in Progress* (Paris: Shakespeare & Co., 1929)
- Published a full decade before the completed novel, this collection of twelve essays was written by Joyce's contemporaries (including Samuel Beckett, William Carlos Williams, and Stuart Gilbert) and two "letters of protest."
- Beckett's essay, "Dante... Bruno. Vico.. Joyce," famously outlined the Viconian cyclical structures and the phonetic logic underpinning the text. It is the foundational critical document.
- [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/ourexagminationr0000unse)

### Joseph Campbell & Henry Morton Robinson — *A Skeleton Key to Finnegans Wake* (1944)
- *A Skeleton Key to Finnegans Wake* (Harcourt Brace, 1944)
- The first book-length attempt to provide a narrative reconstruction and guide to the completed text.
- Interpreted the work heavily through the lens of comparative mythology and Vico's philosophy.
- Though some modern scholars view it as reductive (it imposes too linear a narrative on a fundamentally non-linear text), it remains an essential primer.

### James S. Atherton — *The Books at the Wake* (1959)
- *The Books at the Wake: A Study of Literary Allusions in James Joyce's Finnegans Wake* (Faber, 1959; republished Southern Illinois UP, 1974)
- Atherton shifted the focus to a philological approach, cataloging the staggering array of literary sources and allusions—from sacred texts like the Egyptian *Book of the Dead* to Swift and Carroll—that Joyce wove into the novel.
- Indispensable for understanding the intertextual architecture.

### Clive Hart — *Structure and Motif* (1962)
- *Structure and Motif in Finnegans Wake* (Northwestern University Press, 1962)
- Provided a crucial structural mapping of the book, analyzing its complex leitmotifs and the architecture of its intersecting cycles and dreams.
- Established the definitive Viconian four-book framework that remains the consensus structural reading.

### Adaline Glasheen — *A Third Census of Finnegans Wake* (1977)
- *A Third Census of Finnegans Wake* (University of California Press, 1977)
- An indispensable, exhaustive character index that traces the shifting identities and portmanteau names of the novel's fluid cast.
- Based on three decades of close reading; revised from her First and Second Censuses.

### Roland McHugh — *Annotations to Finnegans Wake* (1980, 4th ed. 2016)
- *Annotations to Finnegans Wake*, 1st ed. (Johns Hopkins University Press, 1980); 4th ed. (2016).
- Considered the definitive line-by-line apparatus. McHugh maps glosses and etymologies directly to the standard page and line layout of the Viking/Faber editions, allowing readers to read the book and the annotations side-by-side.
- Updated in each edition to incorporate new scholarship.

### John Bishop — *Joyce's Book of the Dark* (1986)
- *Joyce's Book of the Dark: Finnegans Wake* (University of Wisconsin Press, 1986)
- Bishop offered a groundbreaking somatic and physiological reading of the text, arguing that the *Wake* is an accurate representation of the sleeping body.
- Central thesis: the book is structured like the Egyptian *Book of the Dead*, charting the dreamer's descent into the unconscious "underworld" of sleep.
- Essential for understanding the Egyptian intertexts.

### David Hayman — *The Wake in Transit* (1990)
- *The Wake in Transit* (Cornell University Press, 1990)
- Hayman pioneered genetic Joyce studies, tracing the compositional history of the *Wake* through Joyce's drafts, notebooks, and early sketches.
- Introduced the concept of "the Arranger," a figure mediating between author and text.

## 2. The Ten Hundred-Letter Thunderclaps

According to the philosophy of Giambattista Vico (*La Scienza Nuova*), the sound of thunder was the primal trigger for human consciousness, language, and religion. Joyce used ten massive "thunderwords" (each 100 letters long, except the last, which is 101) to mark major transitions or cyclical falls in history.

Together, they total **1,001 letters**, mirroring *One Thousand and One Nights* and symbolizing infinite recirculation.

| Thunder | Location | Letter Count | Thematic Context |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **1** | FW 3.15-17 | 101 | The primordial Fall of humanity / Finnegan's fall from the ladder |
| **2** | FW 23.05-08 | 100 | The Tower of Babel and linguistic confusion |
| **3** | FW 44.20-21 | 100 | The fall of the patriarch / societal collapse |
| **4** | FW 90.31-34 | 100 | The fall into history and conflict |
| **5** | FW 113.09-11 | 100 | The fall of human relationships / sexual fall |
| **6** | FW 139.14-16 | 100 | The closing of the first cycle of human ages |
| **7** | FW 257.27-28 | 100 | The fall of the artist / spatial and temporal collapse |
| **8** | FW 314.08-09 | 100 | The fall into the pub (the modern commercial world) |
| **9** | FW 332.05-06 | 100 | The culmination of technological/historical conflict |
| **10** | FW 424.20-22 | 101 | The final thunder/recirculation, triggering the next cycle |

### Scholarly Interpretations

- **W.Y. Tindall**, *A Reader's Guide to Finnegans Wake* (Farrar, Straus & Giroux, 1969): Each thunderword is a multilingual portmanteau containing the word for "thunder" in dozens of languages (French *tonnerre*, Italian *tuono*, Japanese *kaminari*, Arabic *ra'ad*, Hindi *kadak*, German *Donner*, Greek *brontē*).
- **Eric McLuhan**, *The Role of Thunder in Finnegans Wake* (University of Toronto Press, 1997): Analyzes the ten thunders as a structural grammar of the book.
- **Clive Hart**: Demonstrated how the ten thunders serve as structural markers partitioning the overarching Viconian architecture.

## 3. The Euclidean Diagram at FW Page 293 (Dolph's Geometry)

Page 293 contains the **only visual diagram printed in the original 1939 first edition**. It appears during the "Nightlessons" chapter (II.2, pp. 260–308), where Dolph (Shem) is teaching his brother Kev (Shaun) about geometry.

### What the Diagram Shows

The diagram is a direct mock-up of Euclid's *Elements*, Proposition 1 (Book I): constructing an equilateral triangle using a ruler and compass by drawing two overlapping circles.

- **The Geometry:** Two circles overlap, creating the classical *vesica piscis* shape in their intersection.
- **The Symbolism (The ALP Triangle):** The overlapping central region forms a vaginal, ovoid shape. Dolph is essentially drawing a map of their mother's (ALP's) body—"the mudder's" anatomy. The triangle's vertices relate to ALP (A) and other characters.
- **Sacred Geometry & The Conjunctive:** Beyond anatomy, it represents the *coincidentia oppositorum* drawn from Giordano Bruno—the intersection where opposites merge. It serves as a spatial map of the book's cyclical, merging nature.
- **Scholarship:** Roland McHugh's annotations dissect the geometrical puns on this page; Fritz Senn and Laurent Milesi have written extensively on how the diagram visualizes Joyce's mathematical and spatial logic. It is the book's only concession to visual communication outside of language.

## 4. Digital Humanities & Concordance Projects

Modern digital tools have revolutionized the study of the *Wake's* dense textual network.

### FWEET
- **Finnegans Wake Extensible Elucidation Treasury** — [http://www.fweet.org](http://www.fweet.org)
- Created by Raphael Slepon.
- Contains over **100,000 annotations**, etymologies, and glosses compiled from decades of scholarship.
- Fully searchable and linked to specific page/line references.

### James Joyce Digital Archive (JJDA)
- [https://jjda.ie](https://jjda.ie)
- Edited by Hans Walter Gabler and Danis Rose.
- An interactive archive tracing the genetic compositional history of Joyce's works through manuscripts and notebooks.

### The Brepols Buffalo Notebooks Edition
- Ongoing, multi-volume print and digital genetic transcription of the 48 notebooks Joyce used while composing the text.
- Edited by Vincent Deane, Daniel Ferrer, and Geert Lernout.
- [Brepols Publishers](https://www.brepols.net)

### Genetic Joyce Studies
- Leading electronic journal dedicated to the genetic analysis of Joyce's drafts and compositional methods.
- [https://www.geneticjoycestudies.org](https://www.geneticjoycestudies.org)

### Computational Approaches
- *Wake2vec*: Computational embedding projects analyze the semantic drift and neologisms within the text.
- Open-source TEI corpora on GitHub for textual analysis.

## 5. Copyright History & the 2035 U.S. Public Domain Date

The copyright status of *Finnegans Wake* has been a frequent subject of legal and scholarly contention.

- **The U.S. Copyright Term:** Under the 1998 Sonny Bono Copyright Term Extension Act (CTEA), works published in the U.S. between 1923 and 1977 receive protection for 95 years from the date of publication.
- **The 2035 Public Domain Date:** Because *Finnegans Wake* was published on **May 4, 1939**, it will enter the U.S. public domain on **January 1, 2035**. (It entered the public domain in the EU in **2012**, 70 years after Joyce's death in January 1941.)
- **The Estate and Access:** The James Joyce Estate, previously managed aggressively by his grandson Stephen James Joyce, was notorious for restricting access to manuscripts, blocking multimedia projects (including the Michael Groden digital Ulysses/Wake hypermedia project), and fiercely opposing digital humanities initiatives.
- **Fair Use:** Scholars relied on "fair use" provisions to quote from the text for scholarly commentary. The strict copyright enforcement remained a significant impediment until the EU restrictions lapsed.
- **Design Implication:** This is why the WinnegansFake project stores annotations as metadata glosses rather than reproducing any copyrighted text—annotations map to `PPP.LL` coordinates, preserving scholarly utility without reproduction.

## 6. The Ballad of Tim Finnegan & Irish Sources

The title of the novel derives from a mid-19th-century Irish-American comic vaudeville ballad.

- **The Plot of the Ballad:** The song tells of Tim Finnegan, a hod-carrier (bricklayer's assistant) with a love for whiskey. He falls from a ladder, breaks his skull, and is presumed dead.
- **The Wake and Resurrection:** At his wake, a brawl breaks out, and whiskey (*uisce beatha*, the "water of life") is accidentally splashed on his corpse. The whiskey revives him, and he rises to join the party.
- **Joycean Transformation:** Joyce removed the apostrophe from the title to signify that all the "Finnegans" of the world wake up. He transformed this pub song into a universal myth of death, resurrection, and the cyclical nature of human history.

## 7. Publication History of *Finnegans Wake*

The gestation and publication of the novel were an arduous, highly public seventeen-year process.

- **Composition (1922–1939):** Joyce began writing in 1923 immediately after completing *Ulysses*. For over a decade, the book was simply referred to as *Work in Progress*.
- **Serialization in *transition*:** The work was serialized extensively in the Paris-based avant-garde magazine *transition*, edited by Eugene Jolas. Eighteen issues featured Joyce's fragments, inviting both bafflement and early defenses such as those in *Our Exagmination*.
- **Joyce's Blindness:** Throughout the gestation, Joyce suffered from severe eye conditions (glaucoma, cataracts, uveitis). He often had to write on large sheets of paper with thick crayons or dictate text to assistants (including Samuel Beckett) when nearly completely blind.
- **First Publication:** The completed book was published simultaneously by **Faber & Faber** (London) and **Viking Press** (New York) on **May 4, 1939**, two years before Joyce's death on January 13, 1941.
- **Textual History:** A list of corrigenda was published in 1945. Revised editions appeared from Viking in 1958 (incorporating corrections). The Penguin Modern Classics edition and the current standard Viking/Faber editions trace to these revisions. A heavily revised "corrected edition" by Danis Rose (Houyhnhnm Press, 2010) remains controversial.
