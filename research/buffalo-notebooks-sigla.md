# The Buffalo Notebooks and the Sigla System in *Finnegans Wake*

---

## 1. The Buffalo Notebooks

James Joyce's compositional process for *Finnegans Wake* heavily relied on a series of working notebooks. The most significant collection is the **Buffalo Notebooks** preserved at the Poetry Collection, University at Buffalo (SUNY).

- **Catalog:** Cataloged as MSS VI.B.1 through VI.B.50.
- **The Brepols Genetic Editions:** Edited by Vincent Deane, Daniel Ferrer, and Geert Lernout, *The Finnegans Wake Notebooks at Buffalo* (Turnhout: Brepols, 2001–present) provide fully transcribed and annotated genetic editions of these documents.
- **Colored Crayons:** Joyce utilized a unique method of genetic accounting. As he transferred words and phrases from the notebooks into his manuscript drafts, he crossed them out with colored crayons (orange, blue, green, red, brown, black). The Brepols "Abstract of Draft Usage" categorizes exactly which color corresponds to which draft stage and chapter, enabling scholars to reconstruct his working sequence.
- **Roland McHugh's Contribution:** In his seminal 1976 work, *The Sigla of Finnegans Wake* (University of Texas Press), Roland McHugh argued that to understand the fragmented text of the Wake, one must map the structural "sigla" Joyce used in these notebooks rather than rely on fluid character names.

---

## 2. The Complete Sigla System

Joyce developed a shorthand of geometric symbols, known as **sigla**, to designate the fluid characters and structural archetypes of the novel. These appear extensively in the VI.B notebooks.

| Siglum | Symbol | Character | Manuscript Origin / Context |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **∐ / ⊏⊐** | Rotated E / m-shape | HCE (Humphrey Chimpden Earwicker) | VI.B.3; ubiquitous as the central builder |
| **Δ** | Greek Delta | ALP (Anna Livia Plurabelle) | Used heavily as the delta of the Liffey |
| **⊏** | Opening Bracket | Shem the Penman (the writer) | Represents the introverted son |
| **⊐** | Closing Bracket | Shaun the Post (the deliverer) | Represents the extroverted, forward-facing son |
| **⊣** | Reversed L / T-shape | Issy (Isolde) and the 28 Rainbow Girls | Split personality / reflection / 29th leap year day |
| **⊥** | Inverted T | Mamalujo (The Four Old Men / Evangelists) | Four figures holding up the bed/history |
| **S** | S / Snake | The Twelve Customers / Jurors / Apostles | The societal chorus |
| **X** | Cross / Intersection | The Foursome / The Crossroads | Convergence of the Four |

*(Other sigla include O for the twelve, T for Tristan, and Λ for the Shaun/Shem synthesis.)*

---

## 3. HCE (Humphrey Chimpden Earwicker)

HCE is the archetypal male figure of the book, representing the universal father, builder, and sinner.

- **Acronym Expansions:** "Here Comes Everybody", "Haveth Childers Everywhere" (the title of III.3), "Howth Castle and Environs" (first paragraph of the book), and many others scattered throughout the text.
- **Topographic Embodiment:** HCE literally spans the Dublin landscape from Howth Head (his head) to Castleknock (his toes) in Phoenix Park. He is the sleeping giant whose body *is* the city.
- **The Phoenix Park Guilt:** HCE's fundamental but ambiguous "sin" involves an incident of voyeurism or exhibitionism in Phoenix Park involving two girls (Issy split) and observed by three soldiers (a triadic witness structure recalling the Viconian age of heroes).
- **Norse Mythology:** He is frequently associated with Norse invaders like "Harold" or Olaf the White, synthesizing Dublin's Viking history with the archetypal barbarian invader who becomes domesticated.
- **Prototypes:** Adam (the Fall), Noah (flood and drunkenness), Sir Amory Tristram (Norman invader), the Duke of Wellington (victor of Waterloo), Finn MacCool (the sleeping giant of Irish mythology), Charles Stewart Parnell (the fallen political leader), and Tim Finnegan (the bricklayer who falls from a ladder in the ballad).

---

## 4. ALP (Anna Livia Plurabelle)

ALP is HCE's wife, the archetypal female, and the River Liffey personified.

- **River Identity:** She embodies the Liffey and contains the names of hundreds of global rivers within her famous washerwomen chapter (I.8). Joyce famously spent months inserting river names into this chapter.
- **The Midden Letter:** ALP is the author/source of the elusive "midden letter," dug up from a dung heap by a hen named Biddy Doran. This letter—about which the entire novel is an elaborate commentary—represents the text of *Finnegans Wake* itself.
- **The Washerwomen Chapter (I.8, pp. 196–216):** Two washerwomen scrub HCE's dirty clothes in the Liffey, gossiping about ALP's life. As night falls, they transform into an elm tree and a stone—echoing the Bruno dialectic of organic and inorganic principles.
- **Final Monologue (Book IV, pp. 619–628):** ALP delivers a moving final monologue as the old, tired river flowing back out to her "cold mad feary father," the sea, in the book's famous closing sentence that loops back to page 3.
- **Frank Budgen's Account:** Joyce famously told Budgen while composing: *"I am making an engine with only one wheel. No spokes of course. The wheel is a perfect square."*

## 5. Shem & Shaun (The Divided Sons)

The twin sons of HCE and ALP represent the fundamental dualities of humanity, engaging in a continuous Giordano Bruno dialectic of opposition and identification.

- **Shem the Penman:** The exiled, introverted, artistic creator (representing Joyce himself). He famously writes the history of the world on his own body using ink made from his bodily secretions. He is associated with the Elm tree, with writing, exile, and the left.
- **Shaun the Post:** The extroverted, conformist, hypocritical postman who delivers Shem's messages without understanding them. In Book III, he undergoes four manifestations: Shaun, Jaun, Yawn, and ultimately HCE reborn. He is associated with the Stone, with delivery, public approval, and the right.
- **The Bruno Dialectic:** At the extremes of their opposition, they become each other. Shem and Shaun are the same person at different poles of existence—the *coincidentia oppositorum* made flesh.
- **Structural Parallels:** Jacob and Esau, Castor and Pollux, Cain and Abel, Lucifer and Michael, Mutt and Jute, Mookse and Gripes.

---

## 6. Issy & the 28 Rainbow Girls

Issy is the daughter figure, a symbol of youthful seduction and fragmented identity.

- **Identities:** She embodies Isolde (of Tristan and Isolde), Stella, Vanessa (from Swift's life), Alice in Wonderland (Lewis Carroll's child muse), and the temptress figure of the Prankquean.
- **The 28 Rainbow Girls:** She is often accompanied by 28 girls representing the days of February (the leap year month). Issy is the 29th, the extra leap-year day that disrupts the calendar.
- **Mirror Motif:** Suffering from a split personality, she speaks to her mirror reflection (her "shadow" self), which is simultaneously her seductive double and her repressed alter ego.
- **Footnotes in Nightlessons (II.2):** In the homework chapter, Issy provides the flippant, romantic, and cynical footnotes to the academic text produced by her brothers, operating at a lower register of the page layout.

---

## 7. Mamalujo (The Four Old Men)

"Mamalujo" is an amalgamation of **Mat**thew, **Ma**rk, **Lu**ke, and **Jo**hn.

- **The Four Evangelists:** Representing the Gospel writers, but also the four Irish provinces (Ulster, Munster, Leinster, Connacht), four bedposts, four compass directions, and four squawking gulls flying over the ship of Tristan and Isolde.
- **Annals of the Four Masters:** They function as the chroniclers of Irish history (*Annála Ríoghachta Éireann*, compiled 1632–1636 by four Franciscan friars in Donegal).
- **Senile Voyeurism:** They act as impotent voyeurs, constantly watching the younger generation's romances (especially Tristan and Isolde) while reminiscing about the past.

---

## 8. The Twelve Customers

Represented by the siglum **S**, the Twelve are the ubiquitous societal chorus.

- **Functions:** The twelve jurors at HCE's trial, the twelve signs of the zodiac, the twelve apostles, and the twelve regular customers at HCE's pub in Chapelizod.
- **Role:** They function as a Greek chorus of middle-class Dublin gossip, expressing public opinion, casting judgment, and driving the societal rumor mill around HCE's fall.
